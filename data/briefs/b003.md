## Context
TransVale operates about 220 trucks carrying cargo between industrial sites. Each truck has a GPS tracker from one of three different vendors, and dispatchers currently look at three separate vendor portals. Management wants a single view, plus reports on late deliveries and fuel use.

## Scope
- Ingest position data from the three vendor APIs, one of them by webhook and two by polling.
- Real-time map of the fleet with truck status (moving, stopped, offline).
- Alerts when a truck leaves its planned route or stays stopped for more than 30 minutes.
- Daily and monthly reports: on-time delivery rate, kilometers, fuel per route.
- Role-based access for dispatchers, managers and the finance team.

## Constraints
Positions arrive every 10 to 30 seconds per truck. The system must work 24/7 and tolerate a vendor API being down. The client had no data engineers, so the solution needed to be operable by a small IT team. Delivery in two phases, map first, reports second.

## Outcome
Delivered in 1,400 hours against an estimate of 1,100. The three vendor APIs behaved very differently: one had undocumented rate limits, one sent timestamps in local time without a timezone, and one changed its payload format once during the project. Normalizing the data into one model took about 200 hours more than planned. The real-time map was done within the first phase as expected. The fuel report was descoped halfway because one vendor did not expose fuel sensors. The client is happy with the map and the late-delivery alerts. Lesson: budget extra time for every external API, and ask for sample payloads before the estimate.
