## Context
A plastics manufacturer runs 40 injection molding machines. Unplanned stops cost them heavily. They want to collect machine sensor data, detect early signs of failure and plan maintenance before breakdowns.

## Scope
- Collect sensor data from machine controllers (several protocols) into a central store.
- Dashboards with machine status and history.
- Anomaly detection models for temperature, pressure and vibration.
- Alerts to the maintenance team and a maintenance log.
- Pilot on 10 machines, then extend to all 40.

## Constraints
Machines are from five manufacturers and some are more than 15 years old. Factory network is isolated and changes need approval from the plant's IT. No labeled failure history existed.

## Outcome
Delivered in 1,900 hours against an estimate of 1,200, a large overrun. Getting data out of the old controllers took about 500 hours: custom protocol adapters, network approvals and sensors that had to be installed. Without labeled failures we could only build alert rules and simple anomaly detection, not a true failure predictor. The client accepted this after the pilot. Lesson: industrial data projects spend most of the effort on data acquisition, and the machine learning promise must be reduced when there are no labels.
